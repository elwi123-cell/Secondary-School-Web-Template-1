WHO WE ARE

A school at the heart of a growing city

Lae Secondary School is part of the public education landscape of Lae, Papua New Guinea’s major industrial and commercial city.

Public reporting has described Lae Secondary as one of the city’s established schools. Over the years, the school has been associated with large student cohorts, national examination participation, investment in learning facilities, and student involvement in international robotics activities.

The school’s development story includes work supported by its governing structures, families, education authorities and Lae City Authority. Reports from 2019 and 2023 document major infrastructure projects intended to strengthen the environment for teaching and learning.

This profile presents documented milestones rather than unverified claims about the school’s founding date, present enrolment or current status as a School of Excellence.

INNOVATION & REPRESENTATION

Robotics beyond the classroom

International robotics participation has become a notable part of the school’s publicly reported student story.

From Lae to international competition

Public reporting records Lae Secondary School students taking part in robotics competitions overseas. These experiences can give learners opportunities to practise teamwork, design, coding, testing, communication and problem-solving in a competitive setting.

In 2023, reports described the school’s team placing 43rd among 191 countries at a global robotics challenge in Singapore. In 2025, the team returned from Panama City, where it was reported as ranking 90th among 181 countries.

These results are included as reported by news sources. Competition names, divisions and ranking methodologies can differ by event, so the figures should be read in their original reporting context.

PARTNERSHIP & COMMUNITY

School development is a shared effort

Published accounts highlight the contribution of school governance, parents, teachers, public education officials and Lae City Authority to campus development. In 2019, school board leadership discussed the governing council’s approach to delivering infrastructure; in 2023, school leadership acknowledged parents and public authorities for their support.

Families and community partners are important to a safe, supportive learning environment. Current partnership opportunities, parent-association arrangements and school governance contacts should be confirmed directly with the school.

A SHARED COMMITMENT

For students. For families. For Lae.

  • Support consistent attendance and learning.
  • Encourage respect, responsibility and student wellbeing.
  • Value academic achievement and practical innovation.
  • Work together to maintain a safe and supportive school environment.
